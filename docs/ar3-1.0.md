# ar3 1.0 — definition and acceptance criteria (DRAFT for ratification)

Status: draft 2026-09-03, drafted against `VERSION` 0.1.83, awaiting the owner's
ruling. Edit down, do not build up.

Tags for grep: `[CANDIDATE]` — a choice between drafted options. `[RULING
NEEDED]` — a question only the owner answers. `[NEW ISSUE]` — work with no issue
behind it yet.

The bar this page is written against, from the owner's 2026-08-02 scoping memo
§11:

> "I wouldn't consider it 1.0 until I could hand it to someone and we have a
> story for [it] … we don't have to boil the ocean, but we do need to give them
> a fire, so that if they want to boil the ocean, here's our story."

That memo names three gates: **the mission statement and one-pager**,
**integrations and devices ready**, and **the ability to communicate**. They are
G1, G2 and G3 below. Every criterion under them is an observable a second party
can pass or fail without asking the author.

The whole page is subordinate to the standing rule that a thing is finished when
the problem is sufficiently solved for an end user, not when it stops having
flaws.

---

## 1. The 1.0 sentence

**Ruled 2026-09-03 by the owner: the hand-off.**

> *1.0 is the version a person who has never met the author can install from
> one public URL, describe a week of work to, walk away from, and be reached
> by on a channel they already own — with a story on file for what it is and
> why.*

His why, verbatim: *"The hand-off supports my original impetus to regain
attention and peace of mind. It is the executive seat written into our value
proposition."* Every clause is testable by a stranger, and the 2026-08-02 bar ("hand it to
someone and we have a story") is itself a hand-off bar. Two alternatives were
considered and not chosen: the capital ("turns one AI subscription into a team
a person can direct"), which leads with the supporting line the 2026-08-01
positioning ruling keeps out of the lead; and the fire ("the smallest complete
fire: one roster, one project, one reachable human, one page"), which names
the scope but not the test.

## 2. The three gates

### G1 — The mission statement and the one-pager

> "the mission statement and one-pager" — 2026-08-02 §11, the owner's first
> named gate.

- [ ] **G1.1** A stranger reading the public repo's front page states in one
      sentence who it is for and what it gives back, without opening a second
      page.
  - exists: `README.md` opens with a mechanism paragraph — a8s routes, r4t
    governs, k7e keeps. It answers *what*, not *for whom*.
  - missing: #3, the one-pager rewrite. Open, north-star, unstarted.
  - attests: two readers who have not seen ar3 write the sentence
    independently, each citing the URL and pasting their sentence. Agreement is
    the pass.

- [ ] **G1.2** A mission statement exists as a public artifact, not only as
      source.
  - exists: the private message page is validated, and states that it is source
    code and not the artifact.
  - missing: [NEW ISSUE] *publish the mission statement — one public paragraph
    compiled from the message page*, child of #3.
  - attests: the seat attests the public paragraph is derivable line by line
    from the source page, citing both.

- [ ] **G1.3** Every public claim about behaviour is measured, not nominal.
  - exists: the standard is set by the 2026-08-30 cadence correction — a
    published cadence reads "every few minutes", never the configured interval.
  - missing: [NEW ISSUE] a `tools/` claim sweep over `README.md`, `docs/*.md`
    and `guide/`. It goes in `tools/` and runs at release, because a repo-wide
    check placed inside one app's suite is green by not running.
  - attests: the tool's output, zero unmeasured claims, cited by run.

- [ ] **G1.4** The one-pager reads as a free sample: costs nothing, takes a
      minute, leaves the reader wanting more.
  - exists: the framing and the reader profile are written in the one-pager
    brief.
  - missing: the objection ledger — trust, confidentiality, efficiency, staying
    on target — is unwritten. [NEW ISSUE] child of #3.
  - attests: the owner reads it once and says yes or no. Not delegable.

- [ ] **G1.5** The story survives being retold by someone who was not there: a
      reader can say what problem it solves and name one failure it prevents.
  - exists: the worked example in §4 below is the first candidate failure.
  - missing: nothing beyond G1.1 and G1.4; listed so the one-pager is written
    with a failure in it, not only a promise.
  - attests: the same two readers as G1.1 each name one failure, unprompted.

### G2 — Integrations and devices ready

> "the only thing you have to connect is your rig, which you already have
> subscriptions for" — 2026-08-02 §4, on what a new user should have to do.

- [ ] **G2.1** The persona installs from the public one-liner on macOS **and**
      on Windows, and `ar3 doctor` names every unmet prerequisite with a remedy
      true on their machine.
  - exists: `get.sh` ships; `ar3 doctor` probes claude, agent, codex, copilot,
    opencode, ollama, docker and git (`apps/ar3/cli.py`); Windows reached
    minimally working in 0.1.78.
  - missing: #164 Windows smoke job in `release.yml`, #165 `get.ps1`, #177
    doctor probes the user Path, #195 installer reads as short as a one-line
    competitor, #200 and #201 Windows test suites.
  - attests: each failure **forced**, not read from the code. Cite the
    forced-failure run per probe.

- [ ] **G2.2** The bridge from installed subscriptions to rigs is auto-detected,
      not configured.
  - exists: `ar3 doctor` detects harnesses; r4t presets cover six engines
    (`docs/r4t-engine.md`).
  - missing: the 2026-08-02 §4 picture has no verb. [NEW ISSUE] `r4t rig
    detect` — one command that turns detected harnesses into a working rig.
  - attests: a stranger runs one command, gets a working rig, cites the output.

- [ ] **G2.3** Nothing that parses is unwired.
  - exists: the rule, from the 0.1.71 value audit — `Rituals` and `Cells`
    shipped parsed, validated and documented with no scheduler, no dispatch
    path and no state file behind them, and the default runbook promised a
    standup that could not happen. Suite-wide acceptance: every configuration
    key, CLI verb and runbook section that parses is either wired end to end
    with a test that fails when the wiring is removed, or marked deferred in the
    surface where the user meets it. No third category.
  - missing: [NEW ISSUE] the enumerating tool in `tools/` that reports each
    surface as wired, deferred or unaccounted. Highest-value 1.0 item, because
    it converts "done" from a judgement into a count.
  - attests: the tool reports zero unaccounted; cite the run and the commit.

- [ ] **G2.4** A second device — the phone — can approve an irreversible act.
  - exists: nothing shipped.
  - missing: #109, the approval queue: draft, approve from the phone, then act.
    North-star.
  - attests: the owner approves one real act from a phone; cite the queue record
    and the acting commit.

- [ ] **G2.5** A long-running seat states its own context condition: how full it
      is, how long it has run, and when it was last cleared — and a user can
      clear it from the device they are holding.
  - exists: nothing. r4t's pacing and k7e's store address the cause; neither
    reports the condition.
  - missing: [NEW ISSUE] *a seat reports and can reset its own context state,
    from any device it is reachable from*. This is the criterion the 2026-09-03
    example turns on; see §4 bullet 3.
  - attests: a seat that has run a week reports its condition unprompted in a
    scheduled report, and a reset issued from a phone takes effect; cite both
    records.

- [ ] **G2.6** No single host takes the whole team down. Seats run on more than
      one host, and the loss of one host is detected and named within one
      interval rather than discovered by a human.
  - exists: a8s is filesystem-based and already runs seats on separate hosts;
    the reaper and the ULID plus mtime rule handle stale state.
  - missing: [NEW ISSUE] *host-loss detection: a seat whose host stops is
    reported as down, with the time it stopped*. Related to #223.
  - attests: kill one host mid-run; another seat names it as down, with a
    timestamp, before a human asks. Cite the log line and the kill time.

### G3 — The ability to communicate

> "the one common denominator in communication is still email. Email and
> phone." — 2026-08-02 §10.

RCS and telephony are parked by that same paragraph. The 1.0 communication gate
is **email in and out, plus one push channel**, and nothing more.

- [ ] **G3.1** The persona receives a report by email and replies to it, and the
      reply reaches the roster — using only the mail account they already have.
  - exists: the Gmail connector is bidirectional
    (`apps/a8s/connectors/gmail/`, `docs/a8s.md` §connectors).
  - missing: it depends on a personally deployed Apps Script bridge in a
    separate repo. There is no path a stranger can follow and no
    `docs/a8s-email.md`. [NEW ISSUE] *email is a first-class a8s channel: one
    documented setup a stranger can complete*.
  - attests: a stranger sets it up from public docs alone and completes one
    round trip; cite both message ids.

- [ ] **G3.2** A send either arrives or the sender is told it did not.
  - exists: nothing that closes the loop. Delivery failures are invisible to the
    sender, retry records come back empty, and messages park silently.
  - missing: #93, open, updated 2026-09-02. This gates G3 outright — a
    communication gate cannot pass on a channel that fails quietly.
  - attests: force a failure on each configured transport; the sender surfaces
    it every time. Cite the forced runs.

- [ ] **G3.3** A quiet channel can be told from a dead one.
  - exists: the durable-trace rule, established 2026-08-29 for scheduled inbox
    monitors and already in the public recipe. Its origin is a health check that
    wrote and read locally and so passed through a 24-hour dead sync.
  - missing: #223, and the suite-wide extension of the durable-trace rule to
    every scheduled or silent-when-empty mechanism.
  - attests: kill one channel and prove the trace goes stale within one
    interval; cite the log.

- [ ] **G3.4** The report is legible on a walk: a person acts on it from a phone
      without opening a laptop.
  - exists: voice reports exist as a seat ritual (`ares-report`), not as a
    product feature.
  - missing: #213, markdown plus a rendered HTML payload. Speaking naturally is
    parked by 2026-08-02 §10; readable-on-a-phone is not.
  - attests: the owner reads one on a phone and acts on it without a laptop.

- [ ] **G3.5** The human is reachable on a channel they already own even when
      one channel is down: at least two configured channels, and a send that
      names which channel carried it.
  - exists: multiple transports exist; the sender does not report which one
    carried a message, and a dead device is not distinguished from a quiet one.
  - missing: covered in part by #93 and G3.3. [NEW ISSUE] *a delivery record
    names the channel and the device that took the message*.
  - attests: disable the primary channel; the message arrives on the second and
    the record names it. Cite both.

## 3. The master walk — "hand it to someone"

**Persona.** A professional in their 40s or 50s who came up in the
personal-computing boom, still skilled, short on time and focus. Assumed
equipment: a machine, a cloud account, an AI subscription. No prior contact with
the author, no access to the private wiki.

**Path.** Public one-pager → the `get.sh` one-liner from `README.md` → `ar3` →
`ar3 doctor` → guide chapter 1 (`guide/01-hello-agent.md`) → one roster running
→ a report arriving on email or phone → one correction sent back → the roster
acts on it.

**Time box.** 90 minutes of hands-on time.

**Pass condition.** The persona completes the path unassisted inside the time
box and states in one sentence what ar3 does. Any step where they must ask a
question the public docs do not answer is a failed criterion, not a note.

**Attestation.** The shape is the executive loop's: an attestation without its
citation does not count. The tester attests they completed the walk, citing the
commit they installed, the timestamp of the first command and of the last, and
the transcript of every question they had to ask. A walk with no cited
transcript does not count.

**Who attests what.**

| Party | Attests | Citation required |
|---|---|---|
| Outside human tester | The walk, end to end, inside the time box | Commit, first and last timestamps, full question transcript |
| Agent on a harness we do not control | The same walk, and every doc gap it hit | Session log and the diff it proposed |
| The seat | Forced-failure runs for G2.1, G3.2, G3.3, G2.6 | One run per probe or transport, by id |
| `tools/` checkers | G1.3 and G2.3 counts | Run output plus commit |
| The owner | G1.4, and G3.4 read on a phone | His own line; not delegable |

## 4. What 1.0 survives

The worked example is the night of 2026-09-02 into the morning of 2026-09-03,
reported by the owner as a use-case reference. The owner's chief-of-staff bot
ran on one host. That host died overnight, nobody knows when, and it did not
bring itself back. Restoring it was hard; afterwards it was sluggish, its
context filling, with no way to clear it from the phone app. Its daily email
never came. It reported a message to a colleague as sent; the colleague never
received it. A vendor inbox app on the same night did no better. The a8s seats
on that same host came back on their own and the rest of the team stayed
reachable; the k7e seat sent its scheduled reminder on time. The phone seat was
dead — unplugged, signal lost — so one channel to the human was gone.

1.0 must survive each of these. Each bullet names the gate and criterion it
belongs to.

- **A seat host dies overnight and the rest of the team stays reachable.** The
  loss is detected and named with a time, not discovered by a human the next
  morning. → **G2.6**, with **G3.3** proving the difference between a quiet host
  and a dead one.
- **A message the sender believed sent is provably delivered or provably not
  delivered.** No message parks silently, and no agent reports a send it cannot
  evidence. → **G3.2** (#93).
- **A long-running seat does not go foggy, or says when it is.** It reports how
  long it has run and how full it is, and it can be cleared from the device in
  the user's hand. → **G2.5** [NEW ISSUE].
- **The human can be reached on a channel they already have.** When one device
  is dead, a second configured channel carries the message and the record names
  which one did. → **G3.5**, with **G3.1** for the channel everyone already owns
  and **G3.4** for a report that is legible on a phone.
- **The switchboard runs on whatever model the owner picks, local included.**
  The routing and coordination layer is model-agnostic; the answer to an
  exhausted quota is a different engine, not an overage fee. → **G2.2**, with
  the boundary set by ruling 6 in §8: model-agnostic *switchboard* is in 1.0;
  cheaper or local models carrying the *roster's labour* is not.

## 5. M1 / M2 ordering

The prior draft sets four milestones: **M1** the suite tells the truth about
itself, **M2** one real project and one closed loop, **M3** it survives its own
operation, **M4** a stranger can get there. M3 and M4 are downstream of both
orders. The open call is M1 against M2.

**Order M1 → M2 (truth before loop).**
1. A loop built on surfaces that do not work cannot be trusted when it reports
   success.
2. The 0.1.71 audit found three such surfaces at once, so the risk is measured,
   not hypothetical.
3. M1 is the instrument M2 is measured with: every M2 criterion is a claim the
   machinery makes about itself.

**Order M2 → M1 (loop before truth).**
1. 1.0 is a demonstration before it is an artifact; a closed loop is persuasive
   and a tidy surface is not.
2. The owner records not using r4t because it is too complicated (2026-08-16
   §1); a running loop is what fixes that.
3. The 2026-09-03 example is a loop failure, not a surface failure, so it argues
   for the loop first.

**Recommendation: M1 → M2, with one M2 criterion pulled forward.** M1 is small —
one `tools/` enumerator plus a closing sweep — and it is the only milestone
whose completion is a count rather than a judgement. M2's criterion "every
report carries proof" is unmeasurable while unwired surfaces exist. Pull M2.1
(a roster running a week on a machine that is not the owner's laptop) forward to
start in parallel, because it is wall-clock-bound and cannot be compressed
later. The 2026-09-03 note that there have been no long-running experiments is
the reason to start that clock first.

## 6. Date candidates

Cadence evidence: 0.1.80, 0.1.81 and 0.1.82 all merged on 2026-09-02; roughly
one batch every one to three days since 2026-08-13 (`git log`).

**[CANDIDATE] 2026-09-30 — four weeks, about 12 batches.**
Assumes the three-a-day peak is not the average and that eight to twelve batches
land. **Cuts:** G2.4 (#109, phone approval) and G3.4 to 1.x; M2's week-long
unattended run shortened to 72 hours; Windows limited to install, doctor and one
seat, with the test suites deferred. 1.0 is then the hand-off and the story,
with autonomy demonstrated rather than proven.

**[CANDIDATE] 2026-10-31 — eight weeks, about 20 batches.**
Assumes M1 through M4 as written, including the unattended week and the 48-hour
absence, plus Windows parity. **Cuts:** #122 (the Ark's own roster) and #118
(the Ark node) to 1.x; the k7e research arms #193 and #194 stay north-star and
do not gate.

**[CANDIDATE] 2026-12-15 — fifteen weeks.**
Assumes the roster-library shape (2026-08-02 §4) lands inside 1.0. **Cuts:
nothing — and that is the objection.** This date boils the ocean the memo
forbids and collides with the done-beats-perfect rule. Listed so it can be
rejected on the record.

*Recommendation: 2026-09-30 for the declaration, with M2's week-long run
continuing across the line as a 1.0.x criterion.* The hand-off bar is the
owner's stated bar; unattended-week endurance is a property that can keep being
proved after the version number changes.

## 7. Not in 1.0

Each with the line that excludes it. The prior draft's five stand unchanged and
head the list.

- **Multi-user or multi-tenant anything.** k7e is deliberately single-user.
  Prior draft §5.
- **Cheaper or local models carrying the roster's labour.** A 1.x goal: it
  depends on labour being organised well, which is what 1.0 proves. Prior draft
  §5. Restated because it is the most attractive scope creep on the list, and
  because §4's fifth bullet sits next to it — see ruling 6.
- **Migration code of any kind.** Pre-1.0 is scorch-the-earth until the owner
  declares 1.0, and declaring 1.0 is the moment that rule ends. Prior draft §5.
- **The research backlog** moved to the research-backlog reference on
  2026-08-28. Moved, not rejected; none of it gates 1.0. Prior draft §5.
- **The public mirror's feature surface beyond what the recipes need.** Prior
  draft §5.
- **RCS and any telephony provider.** Named and parked by the owner,
  2026-08-02 §10.
- **Taking phone calls and speaking naturally.** Same paragraph — stated as
  direction, not as a gate.
- **The roster library, the "select a company" shape.** 2026-08-02 §4: *"V1 is
  the life assistant"* puts this after V1.
- **Per-agent source-control identity.** 2026-08-02 §9, a research task; no
  G1–G3 observable needs it.
- **Personas.** 2026-08-02 §5 argues for them as a real feature (#234); no
  G1–G3 observable depends on one.
- **The interactive-session economics work** (#236). Already labelled 1.x.
- **Three production use cases as the 1.0 bar.** The 2026-07-15 Decisions row
  *1.0 acceptance is three production use cases* predates the 2026-08-02 memo
  and sets a bar three times the size of one hand-off with a story. **The two
  rulings conflict and only the owner can supersede one — see ruling 5.**

## 8. Rulings needed

1. **RULED 2026-09-03: reading 2.** The lead of the pitch is the hand-off
   itself, in human terms; subscriptions and frugality are scope and support,
   never the lead. The 2026-08-30 relay was a scope statement.
2. **RULED 2026-09-03: the hand-off sentence** (§1). The only one whose every
   clause a stranger can test.
3. **[RULING NEEDED] M1 before M2, or M2 before M1?**
   *Recommend M1 first, with M2.1 started in parallel.*
4. **RULED 2026-09-03: 1.0 ships 2026-09-30.** Email as the one communication
   channel with phone push as the second; macOS install plus Windows install
   and doctor; the unattended-week run crosses the line as a 1.0.x criterion.
5. **RULED 2026-09-03: the hand-off supersedes the 2026-07-15 "three
   production use cases" bar.** His words: "This is the one purpose that ar3
   was built for and it says it efficiently." The three rosters are 1.x proof
   points the story promises, not the gate.
6. **[RULING NEEDED] Is a model-agnostic switchboard inside 1.0, given that
   local models carrying the roster are excluded?** The distinction on offer:
   routing, waking and reporting run on any engine the owner picks, including a
   local one, while the roster's actual labour stays on the subscription
   harnesses.
   *Recommend yes to the switchboard, no to the labour* — it is what the
   2026-09-03 example asks for, and it does not reopen the excluded item.
7. **[RULING NEEDED] Is email the 1.0 communication gate, with phone push as
   the only second channel — yes or no?**
   *Recommend yes*, straight from 2026-08-02 §10.
8. **[RULING NEEDED] Does #93, silent delivery failure, block the 1.0
   declaration — yes or no?**
   *Recommend yes.* A communication gate cannot pass on a channel that fails
   quietly, and the 2026-09-03 example is that failure seen from the
   executive's chair.
9. **[RULING NEEDED] Does 1.0 require Windows, or is macOS-only acceptable with
   Windows stated as 1.1?**
   *Recommend Windows install, doctor and one seat only*, with the test suites
   deferred.
10. **[RULING NEEDED] Is the hand-off tester a real outside person, or an agent
    on a harness we do not control?**
    *Recommend one of each.* The outside-agent walk of 2026-08-29 proved the
    agent half is cheap and finds real defects; the human half is the one the
    owner's bar names.
11. **[RULING NEEDED] Does the context-fog criterion (G2.5) gate 1.0, or is it
    1.x?**
    *Recommend it gates.* It is the failure the owner met first-hand on
    2026-09-03, and a switchboard that degrades without saying so fails the
    hand-off bar on the second week.
12. **[RULING NEEDED] Does declaring 1.0 end scorch-the-earth on the same day?**
    *Recommend yes, and say so in `CLAUDE.md` in the same batch.* That the rule
    ends at the declaration is itself a reason to date the declaration
    deliberately.
