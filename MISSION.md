# Mission

*Draft — this file is the owner's. Edit it freely; everything below is a
starting point, not a proposal to be argued with.*

## Why this exists

The throttle on all AI work is the human brain. Finished work generates new
questions, new issues and new knowledge faster than a person can absorb them,
so speed downstream becomes fatigue upstream. The Ark owes its user rate
control: work dribbled out at the speed a person can eat it.

The people it is for are mid-career and skilled, with a backlog of things they
have wanted to make their whole life and no hours left to make them in. They
already concluded they cannot build those things alone. This answers the
question that follows.

## What done looks like

One person at the apex, directing a roster that holds the depth. They talk to
one agent. The roster carries the rest. The first instruction fits in a
sentence, and everything else defaults.

Not a dashboard to watch. Not autonomy to be nervous about. A fence, and the
freedom is the consequence.

## Current milestone

A life assistant the owner actually runs, and a build-along guide that gets a
reader to the same place on their own machine.

## The rules that outrank convenience

- **Finish.** Done beats perfect; the end user defines done.
- **Elegance over brute force.** Sane defaults, one file, no configuration
  marathons.
- **Never spend the human's attention to save the machine's.**
- **A feature ships only after winning its experiment.**
